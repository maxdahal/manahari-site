KU Brand

Branding · University of Kansas · Jan 2023 to Apr 2024

KU Brand, in questions

The questions a reviewer usually asks about this project, answered one at a time. Scroll through, or jump to the ones you care about.

What was the project?

MD

KU refreshed its identity, and its brand site had to catch up. People struggled to find guidelines and assets on the old site.

I talked with Kansans about what KU meant to them, then helped turn the new identity into a design system and a brand site for campus partners.

What was your role?

MD

I worked as a product designer and frontend developer on a team of 20+ designers, marketers and project managers.

  • UX design and research
  • Frontend development
  • Graphic design

The rebrand was a campus-wide effort with hundreds of contributors. My part was the brand site: research, design and the redesigned pages.

What was wrong with the old site?

MD
  1. An outdated site. The old brand site did not match the new identity.
  2. Hard to find things. KU Communicators had trouble finding clear guidelines and updated assets.

So the goal was a brand site that reflects the new identity and makes guidelines and assets easy to find.

How did you learn what people needed?

MD

I met with Kansans, alumni, students, faculty and staff. I asked open questions about identity, traditions and how they feel about KU, then looked for patterns.

Short conversations worked better than surveys. Small talks gave clearer insights; people rushed through long surveys.

How did you organise all the guidelines?

MD
  1. Map the content. I listed what people look for most.
  2. Group the guidelines. I put related rules together.
  3. Propose layouts. I turned the rules into page layouts and components.

Small focus groups tested early brand elements and layouts, and I watched people use the old site. I used what I saw to refine hierarchy, language and direction.

Why keep the early wireframes grey?

MD

So we discussed structure before color. I started with boxes and added detail one layer at a time; each step answered one question before the next began.

Which design decisions mattered most?

MD
  1. Keep the two core colors. Kansas Blue and Crimson stayed, the colors people already knew.
  2. Name every color. Plain names like Night, Lake and Sky, so everyone used the same words.
  3. Never rely on color alone. Every pairing passed a contrast check first.
  4. Write in the brand voice. Active voice, and “toward” instead of “towards”.

Six decisions in all, including type by job and the grid and frames.

What did testing change?

MD

Focus groups and A/B tests of early concepts showed what was not working yet: tone, the order of importance, and how people would really use some concepts.

Layouts that looked clean in Figma confused real people. We changed them before launch.

What happened after launch?

MD
  • +30%Traffic across ku.edu after launch
  • ~100KVisitors a month across ku.edu and its subdomains

The new identity now sits in one system of logos, colors, type and usage rules. See it live at brand.ku.edu

What didn’t work?

MD
  1. Surveys lacked depth. Many people rushed through them.
  2. People liked the old brand. Many preferred the old fonts, colors and logo: status quo bias and the mere exposure effect.

What would you do the same way again?

MD
  1. Update visuals early. It made the build smoother and more consistent.
  2. Test outside the design tool. Layouts that look clean in Figma can feel hard to use for real people.
