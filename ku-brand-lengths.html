KU Brand

A new brand site for KU Communicators and campus partners, live at brand.ku.edu.

How long do you want to read?

The problem

KU refreshed its identity, but its brand site didn’t match it, and people couldn’t find guidelines or assets.

+30% Traffic across ku.edu after launch
~100K Visitors a month across ku.edu and its subdomains

My role

Product designer and frontend developer, on a team of 20+ designers, marketers and project managers.

What I did

  1. Interviews
  2. Content map
  3. Grey wireframes
  4. Design rules
  5. Testing
  6. Build

Biggest lesson

Test outside the design tool. Layouts that look clean in Figma can feel hard to use for real people.

See it live at brand.ku.edu

01

The problem

The old brand site didn’t match KU’s new identity.

What I did

I helped turn the refreshed identity into one digital system: how logos, colors, typography and spacing show up across the site. Every choice was checked against the brand platform, “Towering Toward the Blue”.

02

The problem

KU Communicators couldn’t find clear guidelines and updated assets.

What I did

  1. Map the content. I listed what people look for most.
  2. Group the guidelines. I put related rules together.
  3. Propose layouts. I turned the rules into page layouts and components.

Short interviews and small focus groups told me what to put first.

03

The problem

The team needed to agree on structure before talking about color.

What I did

I kept the early wireframes grey on purpose and added detail one layer at a time: boxes, then real copy, then the brand frame, then photos and color. Each step answered one question before the next began.

04

The problem

Hundreds of contributors needed to use the brand the same way.

What I did

We wrote six design rules down once. A few of them:

  • Keep the two core colors, Kansas Blue and Crimson.
  • Name every color, like Night, Lake and Sky, so everyone used the same words.
  • Never rely on color alone: every pairing passed a contrast check first.

05

The problem

Layouts that looked clean in Figma confused real people.

What I did

Focus groups and A/B tests of early concepts showed what wasn’t working yet: tone, the order of importance, and how people would really use some concepts. We fixed those before launch.

What it led to

  • +30%Traffic across ku.edu after launch
  • ~100KVisitors a month across ku.edu and its subdomains
  • 6Design rules written down once

What didn’t work

Surveys lacked depth: many people rushed through them. And people liked the old brand: status quo bias and the mere exposure effect.

What I’d keep doing

Update visuals early, which made the build smoother, and test outside the design tool.
