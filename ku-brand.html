The story in short

KU refreshed its identity, and its brand site had to catch up. People struggled to find guidelines and assets on the old site. I talked with Kansans about what KU meant to them, then helped turn the new identity into a design system and a brand site for campus partners. It is live at brand.ku.edu, and traffic across ku.edu grew 30% after launch.

The new platform

“Towering Toward the Blue” comes from a line in KU’s Alma Mater. It became the brand platform for how KU looks, sounds and feels. The logo, Jayhawk and crimson and blue stayed. We checked each design choice against it.

Look

Bold and clear

Strong type, a visible grid and wide space.

Sound

Confident and warm

Active voice. Skyward words like climb, soar and build.

Feel

Proud of Kansas

Real campus photos and the colors people already knew.

Low-fi to high-fi

I started with boxes and added detail one layer at a time. Each step answered one question before the next began. I kept the early steps grey on purpose, so we discussed structure before color.

  1. 1

    Boxes only

    I drew boxes and labels, with no color and no real text. This showed the order and size of each block: the hero, positioning, goal and four guideline cards.

  2. 2

    Grey wireframe

    I added the real navigation, headings and copy. The slogan was the main text in the hero. Now we could check the hierarchy and the reading order.

  3. 3

    Brand frame

    I added the holding box and pinpoints from the graphic elements. They showed how the brand frame sits on the page.

  4. 4

    High fidelity

    I added photos from the Brand Center, Kansas Blue and Crimson. Some layouts looked clean at this stage but felt hard to use, so I kept checking them against the grey versions.

The same steps on two more pages

I used the same steps for the guideline page and the downloads page.

Design system decisions

  1. Decision 1

    Keep the two core colors

    Kansas Blue (#0051ba) and Crimson (#e8000d) stayed, the colors people already knew.

  2. Decision 2

    Name every color

    Each color got a plain name, like Night, Lake and Sky, so everyone used the same words.

  3. Decision 3

    Never rely on color alone

    Every pairing passed a contrast check first. We listed the pairings to use and the ones to avoid.

  4. Decision 4

    Pick type by job

    Roc Grotesk and Moret for headlines. Gotham Narrow or Freight Text for body. KU’s rule set web body text at 12 to 16 px.

  5. Decision 5

    Use the grid and frames

    Square grid units for layouts, a thin Fire line for holding boxes, Lake dots for pinpoints.

  6. Decision 6

    Write in the brand voice

    Active voice, present or future tense, and “toward” instead of “towards”. We kept tower images rare.

Learn

08

What didn’t work

Surveys

Surveys lacked depth

Many people rushed through them.

Familiarity

People liked the old brand

Many preferred the old fonts, colors and logo: status quo bias and the mere exposure effect.

A final note

The rebrand was a campus-wide effort with hundreds of contributors. My part was the brand site: research, design and the redesigned pages.

Takeaways

  1. 01

    Update visuals early

    It made the build smoother and more consistent.

  2. 02

    Test outside the design tool

    Layouts that look clean in Figma can feel hard to use for real people.
