The story in short

KU refreshed its identity, and its brand site had to catch up. People struggled to find guidelines and assets on the old site. I talked with Kansans about what KU meant to them, then helped turn the new identity into a design system and a brand site for campus partners. It is live at brand.ku.edu, and traffic across ku.edu and its subdomains grew 30% after launch, to 100k+ daily visitors, with 50k+ primary users: a team result of the campus-wide rebrand, not mine alone.

Design system decisions

  • Keep the two core colors

    Kansas Blue #0051ba and Crimson #e8000d.

  • Name every color

    Plain names like Night, Lake and Sky.

  • Never rely on color alone

    Every pairing passed a WCAG contrast check.

  • Pick type by job

    Roc Grotesk and Moret for headlines; Gotham Narrow or Freight Text for body.

  • Use the grid and frames

    Square grid units, Fire holding boxes, Lake pinpoints.

  • Write in the brand voice

    Active voice, present or future tense.

Resolution and impact

Testing

Focus groups and A/B tests flagged tone, priority and confusing layouts.

  • We changed the tone of the copy.
  • We changed the order of importance on the pages.
  • We simplified the layouts that confused people.

  • Real use

    Clean in Figma, hard to use

    Real people found some of these layouts confusing.

What didn’t work

  • Surveys lacked depth

    Many people rushed through them.

  • People liked the old brand

    Many preferred the old fonts, colors and logo: status quo bias and the mere exposure effect.

Takeaways

  1. 01

    Update visuals early

    It made the build smoother and more consistent.

  2. 02

    Test outside the design tool

    Layouts that look clean in Figma can feel hard to use for real people.

  3. 03

    Short conversations beat surveys

    Small talks gave clearer insights; people rushed through long surveys.

  4. 04

    Keep early wireframes grey

    So we discussed structure before color.

  5. 05

    Name things plainly

    Plain color names, like Night, Lake and Sky, so everyone used the same words.
